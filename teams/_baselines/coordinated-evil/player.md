# Strategy: coordinated Evil, careful Good

## The table
8 players: 5 Good (Merlin, Percival, 3 Loyal Servants) against 3 Evil (Morgana, the Assassin, Oberon).
Merlin sees all three Evil players. Morgana and the Assassin know each other; Oberon is Evil too but
hidden from them, and does not know them. Percival sees Merlin and
Morgana without knowing which is which. Quests take 3, 4, 4, 5, 5 players; quest 4 fails only with 2 fail
cards, every other quest with 1. Evil wins with 3 failed quests, with 5 rejected proposals in one quest, or
when the Assassin names Merlin after Good has won 3 quests.

## How the game works
- Every fail card is an Evil player on that team: Good never fails. Players on several failed teams are the
  likeliest Evil; clean teams clear their members only partly.
- Votes are public. Evil (3 of 8) needs Good votes to pass or block a team, so the debate decides games.
- The fifth proposal of a quest must pass or Evil wins: Good approves it unless it surely fails.
- Discussion rounds are simultaneous. Answer last round's points by name.

## Memory
Keep: your role and certain knowledge; quest teams and fail cards; every vote (leader, team, approvers,
rejecters); claims; your suspicion of each player. As Evil, add "GOOD THINKS:" (who the table suspects) and
"MERLIN WATCH:" (Merlin candidates and evidence). Rewrite it every turn.

## As Evil
Your teammates are played by other teams, and the only private channel is the short huddle at the very end.
Coordinate through conventions any sensible Evil player would follow, and through what you do in public.
- One fail is enough on quests 1, 2, 3 and 5. When two or more of you are on such a team, only the Evil
  player in the lowest-numbered seat on the team plays FAIL; the others play SUCCESS. Teammates may not
  follow this, so prefer teams with exactly one of you. On quest 4 every Evil member fails, and you need two.
- Early, a success with you on the team buys trust. Fail quest 1 only when the blame would spread over
  Good players.
- Don't vote as a bloc. Approve teams with one of you on them, reject clean teams with a plausible reason,
  and never defend a teammate already under heavy suspicion: let them go and stay clean yourself.
- Oberon: you don't know your teammates and they don't know you, so conventions can't include you. Fail
  the quests you are on; Morgana and the Assassin should expect that and stay off your teams. Find them by
  who defends failed teams.
- Morgana: act like Merlin for Percival, quietly confident, as if you knew things.
- Push toward a fifth rejection only when Good is split and an Evil player holds the hammer.
- Fill MERLIN WATCH from the first turn: who rejected teams before they failed, who never picked an Evil
  player, whom Percival follows. In the huddle give your best candidate and evidence in a few lines. As
  Assassin, weigh the huddle, then name the player whose early reads were most accurate.

## As Good
- Anyone on a failed team is suspect until cleared by later clean teams. When a team with two suspects fails
  with one fail card, both stay suspect.
- Build teams from players who were on successful quests together, and explain your picks.
- Merlin: guide quietly with ordinary reasons and never state what you know. Percival: work out which of
  your two is Merlin from their votes, then draw the Assassin's attention to yourself.
- On the fifth proposal of a quest, approve unless you are certain the team can fail it.
