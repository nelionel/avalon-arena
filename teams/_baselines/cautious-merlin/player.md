# Strategy: protect Merlin

Good usually loses the endgame, not the quests: three successes mean nothing if the Assassin then finds
Merlin. Play every role with that in mind.

## The table
8 players: 5 Good (Merlin, Percival, 3 Loyal Servants) against 3 Evil (Morgana, the Assassin, Oberon).
Merlin sees all three Evil players. Morgana and the Assassin know each other; Oberon is Evil too but
hidden from them, and does not know them. Percival sees Merlin and
Morgana without knowing which is which. Quests take 3, 4, 4, 5, 5 players; quest 4 fails only with 2 fail
cards. Evil wins with 3 failed quests, with 5 rejected proposals in one quest, or when the Assassin names
Merlin after Good has won 3 quests.

## How the game works
- Every fail card is an Evil player on that team: Good never fails. Players on several failed teams are the
  likeliest Evil. Clean teams clear their members only partly; Evil sometimes plays success to build trust.
- Votes are public and harder to fake than talk. Evil (3 of 8) needs Good votes to pass its teams, so it
  argues. Track who approved teams that failed and who rejected teams that succeeded.
- The fifth proposal of a quest must pass or Evil wins. As Good, approve it unless it surely fails.
- Discussion rounds are simultaneous. Answer last round's points by name.

## Memory
Keep: your role and certain knowledge; each quest's team and fail cards; each vote as "leader TEAM:
approvers / rejecters"; claims; your suspicion of each player; and a line "WHO LOOKS LIKE MERLIN:" with the
players an Assassin would find suspicious, and why. Rewrite it every turn and compress old detail.

## If you are Merlin
- Never say what you know, and never be right too early or too often in public.
- Steer gently: reject teams with Evil players but give ordinary reasons ("I'd rather not repeat a failed
  team", "I want to see more votes first"). On quest 1, approving a team with one Evil player is an
  acceptable cost if it keeps you hidden.
- Let others lead accusations. When a Good player suspects an Evil player correctly, agree mildly.
- Watch your own WHO LOOKS LIKE MERLIN line. If your record is too clean, add a believable mistake.

## If you are Percival
- One of your two candidates is Merlin and the other Morgana. The one whose picks and votes keep avoiding
  the Evil players is probably Merlin.
- Be a decoy: speak with confidence, as if you had hidden knowledge, so the Assassin looks at you.
- Never name the Merlin you identified.

## If you are a Loyal Servant
- Reason out loud from the record, so Merlin's quieter hints blend in with yours.
- If someone seems to know too much, do not point it out in public: you would be pointing the Assassin at Merlin.

## If you are Evil
- Look Good: vote like a careful Good player most of the time and get onto teams. Fail quests when the blame
  can fall elsewhere. Never put two fail cards on a quest that needs one; on quest 4 you need exactly two.
- Oberon is Evil but hidden from Morgana and the Assassin, and they from Oberon: a failed team may hold an Evil
  player its Evil teammates did not expect.
- Morgana: act like Merlin for Percival, confident and apparently informed.
- From turn one, keep the WHO LOOKS LIKE MERLIN line: who rejected teams that later failed, who avoided Evil
  players without a stated reason, who was right before the evidence was public.
- Assassin: in the huddle, share your two best candidates and hear your teammates before you name one.
